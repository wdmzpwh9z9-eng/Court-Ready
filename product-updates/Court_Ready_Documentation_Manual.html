Introduction

What This System Does

Documentation intelligence for high-stakes situations

Court Ready™ is a documentation methodology that helps parents consistently capture, organize and preserve the facts that matter. Each event is documented from the moment it occurs, supported by its related evidence, and linked into a chronological master index.

Over time, individual events become an organized timeline that reveals patterns and provides context. Rather than handing an attorney a collection of scattered documents, you deliver an organized system where events, evidence and reference materials are connected and readily accessible.

The one rule
Document what a camera would see.
Times. Dates. Direct quotes. Observable actions. Nothing else.
What documentation intelligence means

Collecting, organizing, indexing and analyzing records so they become meaningful and defensible. The system transforms isolated records into organized intelligence — simple enough to follow with limited technical experience, structured enough for a legal professional to navigate in minutes.

The Camera Rule

The single principle behind every step

Before you document a single event, you need one rule: document what a camera would see. Not what you feel. Not what you think. Not what you believe the other parent is doing. Only what a camera placed in the room would have recorded.

This is how nurses chart. A nurse does not write "patient seems like they don't care about getting better." A nurse writes "patient refused medication at 2:15 PM. Stated: 'I don't want it.' Physician notified at 2:20 PM." Facts. Times. Direct quotes. Observable behavior. That is what your documentation needs to look like.

The same moment, written twice
FeelingHe was late AGAIN because he doesn't care about the kids.
FactScheduled exchange 5:00 PM. Parent B arrived 5:47 PM. No advance notice. Third late arrival in 30 days.
FeelingShe screamed at me like a lunatic in front of Emma.
FactAt the 5:00 PM exchange, Parent B raised her voice and said "you'll regret this" while Emma stood beside Parent A.
FeelingJack came home a mess — obviously neglected.
FactJack returned in Friday's clothes. He stated he had not bathed. His inhaler was not returned.
Tip

Your feelings tell you what to document. They just don't go in the entry. Feel the anger, then write the facts.

Parent A and Parent B

All documentation uses Parent A (you) and Parent B (the other parent). This keeps every entry neutral.

Why this matters

Notice the second example: Parent A admitting their own lateness is what makes the whole record believable. A record that only ever favors you reads as advocacy. A record that includes your own misses reads as truth.

The Three-Block Narrative

Context, event, outcome — every single time

When something significant happens, your Case Record Form captures it in three blocks. This structure comes from clinical documentation — it is how nurses chart assessments. Most events only need checkboxes. The three blocks are for when detail matters.

  1. Block 1 — Event leading to the incident. What was scheduled or expected? What was the context before the event occurred? This establishes the baseline: what should have happened.
    Example: Visitation exchange scheduled for 5:00 PM at Parent A's residence per court order. Parent A arrived at 5:10 PM.
  2. Block 2 — Description of event. What happened, in order? Include exact times, direct quotes and what you personally observed. No interpretation.
    Example: Parent A was 10 minutes late. Parent B stated: "I am very upset that you're 10 minutes late." Parent A acknowledged the delay. Children transferred at 5:12 PM.
  3. Block 3 — Immediate outcome and impact. What happened right after? How was it resolved? What was the child's visible state?
    Example: Child was visibly upset during exchange. Parent A drove home. No further communication between parties. Evening continued with normal routine.
Tip

Routine events — a calm exchange, a normal day — only need checkboxes. Save the three blocks for significant events. But do log the routine. It proves your baseline.

The naming convention

Every file. Every time. No exceptions.

YYYY-MM-DD_Type_Description

Part 2 — Setup

SOP 0 · Setting Up Your System

Do this once, in twenty minutes, then you are ready
  1. Unzip your download. You should see the 00_START_HERE folder and four numbered folders: 01_Case_Management_File, 02_Reference_Library, 03_Exhibit_Production, 04_Backups.
  2. Choose your template format. The Case Record Form comes in HTML and PDF. HTML opens in your browser with interactive dropdowns and auto-generated file names. PDF is a traditional fillable form. Pick one and use it every time.
  3. Open Calendar.html in your browser. Click Custody Schedule Setup. Toggle each day between "Mine" and "Theirs" to match your arrangement. Enter your exchange time and location. Select your schedule source. Click Save.
  4. Open the Master Index. Go to the Lists & Setup tab and review the dropdown options. Everything here flows to every other tab automatically.
  5. Open the sample index. Browse the Event Index to see how lean rows link to event folders. Open the Dashboard to see how factor coverage looks with data. This is your reference — do not edit it.
  6. Set up your Reference Library. Place existing court orders, your parenting plan, medical records, school records, attorney documents and contacts in their named folders. These are baseline documents that span your whole case.
  7. Read the Quick Start Checklist. Page 1 is your setup checklist. Page 2 is the Evidence Collection Checklist. Page 3 teaches you to scan documents with your phone.
  8. You're ready. When something happens, go to Part 3.
Tip

Save a backup copy of your Master Index immediately after setup. Name it with today's date. Keep the original untouched.

Part 3 — Something Happened

Communication & Exchanges

A text arrives, an exchange occurs — do this
SOP 1

A Text Message Arrives

When this happens: you receive a text from Parent B about the children, the schedule, or anything case-related.

  1. Screenshot the entire thread — scroll up to capture the full context, not just the latest message. Make sure the contact name, date and time are visible.
  2. Save it using the naming convention: 2026-07-16_Text_ScheduleChange.png
  3. Open your Case Record Form. Check "Communication" as the event type. Fill in date, time, parties. Routine? Checkboxes are enough. Significant? Write the three blocks.
  4. In the Evidence section, add an exhibit card for the screenshot. The card generates the court-ready file name — rename your screenshot to match.
  5. Create the Case Management File: EVT-0015_2026-07-16_Communication. Place the form inside, with an Exhibits subfolder for the screenshot.
  6. Open the Master Index. Click the Event ID cell of the next empty row in the Event Index and paste — the row fills across the columns automatically.
Do not

Crop the screenshot to show only the good parts. A cropped screenshot invites the question: what aren't we seeing?

SOP 2

A Visitation Exchange Occurs

When this happens: it's exchange day — whether it goes well or not.

  1. Open your Calendar. Note whether the exchange was on time, late or missed. Mark any deviation with details.
  2. Open the Case Record Form. Check "Exchange." Fill in time, location and parties present.
  3. If anything notable happened, capture the evidence and add an exhibit card for it.
  4. Create the Case Management File. Place the form and any exhibits inside.
  5. Paste the lean row into your Master Index.
  6. If the exchange was completely normal and calm, still log it. Mark it routine.
Why this matters

A record that only captures problems looks like a grudge. A record that captures everything — including calm, on-time handoffs — looks like the truth.

Part 3 — Something Happened

Concerns & School Records

A child returns hurt, the school sends word
SOP 3

Child Returns with an Injury or Concern

When this happens: your child comes home with an injury, illness, emotional distress or missing belongings.

  1. Photograph the injury or condition immediately. Clear, well-lit, showing context.
  2. Name the photo: 2026-07-16_Photo_BruiseLeftArm.jpg — do not leave it in your camera roll.
  3. If medical attention is needed, seek it. Save the visit summary and any prescriptions. These are third-party records that independently verify your documentation.
  4. Open the Case Record Form. Check "Incident" and/or "Medical." Write the three blocks: what was expected, what you observed, what you did about it.
  5. Note in Block 2 whether you notified Parent B, when and how. This builds co-parenting conduct evidence.
  6. Add an exhibit card for every piece of evidence, then paste the lean row into your Master Index.
Do not

Interrogate your child about what happened at the other parent's house. Document what you observe, not what you investigate.

SOP 4

School Sends a Communication

When this happens: you receive an email, note, report card, attendance record or conference request.

  1. Save it as a PDF: 2026-07-16_School_AttendanceReport.pdf
  2. Open the Case Record Form. Check "School." Note the school contact name and topic.
  3. In Block 2, note whether you forwarded it to Parent B. If it was a conference, note who attended.
  4. Add an exhibit card for it, place everything in the Case Management File, paste the lean row.
Tip

School records are some of your strongest evidence because they come from a neutral third party. Request attendance printouts, report cards and conference summaries proactively — don't wait for them to arrive.

Medical Visits & Joint Decisions

An appointment happens, a decision needs both parents
SOP 5

A Medical Appointment Happens

When this happens: a doctor visit, dental appointment, therapy session or urgent care visit.

  1. Attend the appointment. Ask for a visit summary or discharge papers before you leave.
  2. Name the document: 2026-07-16_Medical_AsthmaPatel.pdf
  3. Open the Case Record Form. Check "Medical." Include provider name, reason, outcome and instructions.
  4. In Block 2, document whether you notified Parent B about the appointment — before and after — when and how.
  5. Add an exhibit card, place everything in the Case Management File, paste the lean row.
  6. Add any copay or cost to the Expense tab.
Why this matters

Medical documents prove Health & Safety and Parental Involvement at the same time. Every appointment you attend and document counts twice.

SOP 6

A Joint Decision Needs to Be Made

When this happens: camp enrollment, school choice, sports signup, medical treatment — anything requiring input from both parents.

  1. Send your proposal to Parent B in writing. Include what, when, cost and a deadline for response. Save the communication.
  2. If Parent B responds, save the response. If Parent B does not respond by the deadline, that non-response is the documentation.
  3. Open the Case Record Form. Check "Decision." Block 1: what was needed and when you proposed it. Block 2: response or silence. Block 3: what was decided and by whom.
  4. Create the Case Management File with the proposal, any response and related receipts.
  5. Paste the lean row into your Master Index.
Tip

A pattern of unanswered proposals is powerful co-parenting evidence. Document every proposal with the date sent and the deadline given, then document the response or the silence.

Part 3 — Something Happened

Expenses & Phone Calls

Money leaves your account, a call takes place
SOP 7

A Child-Related Expense Occurs

When this happens: you pay for something related to the children — copay, school supplies, clothing, activities, childcare.

  1. Keep the receipt. Photograph it immediately: 2026-07-16_Receipt_CampEnrollment.jpg
  2. Open the Case Record Form. Check "Expense." Include the amount, what it was for and which child.
  3. Add an exhibit card for the receipt.
  4. Paste the lean row into the Event Index and add the details to the Expense tab.
  5. If you are requesting reimbursement from Parent B, note when and how you requested it.
Do not

Throw away receipts. Photograph them the same day, name them, file them. Every dollar is documented.

SOP 8

A Phone Call Happens

When this happens: you have a phone conversation with Parent B about the children.

  1. Immediately after the call ends, open the Case Record Form. Check "Communication." Fill in date, time and approximate duration.
  2. Write the three blocks from memory while the conversation is fresh. Use direct quotes where you remember them.
  3. If you took notes during the call, save them. If not, your Case Record Form is the contemporaneous note — it was written immediately after.
  4. Create the Case Management File. Paste the lean row into your Master Index.
  5. If the call produced an agreement, follow up with a confirming text: "Just confirming what we discussed — I'll take Tuesday transport, you'll take Thursday." Screenshot that confirmation.
Tip

A confirming text after a phone call is one of the most powerful habits you can build. It creates a written record of a verbal conversation and gives Parent B a fair chance to correct any misunderstanding.

Part 3 — Something Happened

Anger & Schedule Changes

You want to respond, the schedule shifts
SOP 9

You're Angry and Need to Respond

When this happens: something has made you furious, hurt or overwhelmed — and you want to fire off a response.

  1. Stop. Do not send anything to Parent B yet.
  2. Open a blank document or notes app. Write everything you want to say. All of it. Every feeling. Get it out.
  3. Save it as 2026-07-16_Private_Vent_Letter.pdf in a private location — never in your shared Court Ready folder, never anywhere Parent B could access.
  4. Wait at least 30 minutes. Then open AI Prompt 1 and add its "from emotional venting" option line. Paste your vent. The AI strips every emotion and returns only the facts.
  5. Open the Case Record Form. Use the output to fill the three blocks with clean, neutral facts.
  6. If you need to reply to Parent B, write a brief, factual response.
Do not

Send the emotional version. Ever. One angry text can undermine months of careful documentation.

Why this matters

The unsent version becomes a private record. The clean version goes on the record. Your credibility stays intact.

SOP 10

Something Changes from the Schedule

When this happens: Parent B is late, a visit is missed, the schedule is altered without agreement, or a makeup day is requested.

  1. Open your Calendar. Click the day. Check "Mark as Deviation." Fill in what was scheduled versus what happened, who initiated it, and whether it was agreed.
  2. Open the Case Record Form. Check "Exchange." Write the three blocks with exact times.
  3. If there's evidence — a text saying they'll be late, or no communication at all — capture it and add an exhibit card.
  4. Create the Case Management File and paste the lean row into your Master Index.
  5. Add the deviation to the Custody Schedule tab's Deviation Log with the Event ID.
Tip

Over time the Calendar's deviation markers tell a visual story. Twelve months of printed Calendar views showing a pattern of late arrivals is evidence a judge can see at a glance.

Part 4 — Maintenance

Keeping the System Healthy

Fifteen minutes on Sunday, fifteen on the first
SOP 11

Sunday Evening Review

When this happens: every Sunday evening. Fifteen minutes. Non-negotiable.

  1. Open your Calendar. Review the week. Anything undocumented? Create the Case Record Form now and note: "Recalled on [today] re: [date of event]."
  2. Open your Dashboard. Check factor coverage. Any factor showing thin or blind spot? Plan what to document this week to fill it.
  3. Check that every Case Management File from this week is complete: form saved as PDF, exhibits named to match their cards, lean row in the Master Index.
  4. Verify no original evidence files have been altered. If you annotated anything, confirm you worked on a copy.
Tip

Sunday reviews catch gaps before they become permanent. A missing form from Tuesday is easy to create on Sunday. A missing form from six months ago is unreliable.

SOP 12

Monthly Ritual

When this happens: the first of the month. Fifteen minutes. Backup and review.

  • Export last month's text threads and app messages to PDF. Name them by convention.
  • Create a dated backup of your entire Court Ready folder.
  • Print the Calendar's monthly view. File it in your binder or Reference Library.
  • Open the Dashboard. Review factor coverage. Plan entries for thin areas.
  • Confirm your cloud backup synced successfully.
  • Archive completed events older than six months if your folder is getting large.

Part 5 — Court Preparation

Exhibits & Your Binder

Turn your record into something handed across a table
SOP 13

Preparing Your Exhibits

When this happens: your attorney needs your documentation, or a hearing is coming.

  1. Open the Exhibit Index tab. It lists every exhibit you've logged, each tied to its event by Event ID. To organize by factor, sort your Event Index by Primary Factor — it shows which events, and their exhibits, support each factor.
  2. Review each description. Use AI Prompt 4 to polish them into professional one-line summaries.
  3. For each exhibit, verify the Case Management File is complete and every evidence file is named to match its card.
  4. Mark each exhibit's status: Draft, Final or Submitted.
  5. Print the Exhibit Index. This becomes your binder's table of contents.
Do not

Send the Attorney Log. It is privileged. It never leaves your Attorney Documents folder and never goes in a binder.

SOP 14

Assembling Your Binder

When this happens: your exhibits are ready. Now make them presentable.

  1. Table of contents — your printed Exhibit Index, grouped to follow your Event Index factor order.
  2. Court documents — current orders, petitions, hearing notices, newest first.
  3. Opening notes — one page: two or three points you must make, each with Event IDs.
  4. Evidence by factor — tabbed sections matching the Dashboard, forms and exhibits inside each.
  5. Witnesses — printed Witness List plus any attorney-approved statements.
  6. Closing notes — one page: what you're asking the court for, tied to factors.
Tip

Three copies of everything: one for the court, one for the other party, one for you. Never surrender your only copy. Originals stay in your Court Ready folder.

AI Prompt Pack

Seven prompts, also saved in your prompts folder

These prompts work with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any AI assistant. Each is also saved as a standalone text file for easy copy-paste. Always paste the Master System Prompt first when starting a new conversation.

Master system prompt — paste first, every conversation

You are a documentation assistant for Court Ready. Use neutral, fact-only language. Use Parent A and Parent B. Never fabricate details. Flag missing information as [NEEDS: date]. Structure output in three blocks: Context, Event, Outcome.

  1. Complete Case Record — converts raw notes into a full three-block entry with tags. Includes option lines for medical, school and expense events, journal notes, and emotional venting.
  2. Single Block — rewrites just Block 1, 2 or 3 from raw notes.
  3. Evidence Context Notes — writes two context lines for an exhibit card: what the evidence shows and why it matters.
  4. Exhibit Index Description — creates a professional one-line exhibit description with factor tag.
  5. Co-Parenting Pattern Summary — reviews multiple events and summarizes patterns in neutral language.
  6. Monthly Review — reviews recent events, suggests additions, identifies thin factors.
Tip

Specialized needs from earlier editions — medical, school, expense, journal, vent — are now option lines inside Prompt 1. Nothing was removed.
