## 2. Brand architecture: the two-level system you asked about

You wanted a "series anchor + per-volume accent" structure. Map it onto what exists:

### Level 1 — The house: Vejovis Intelligence Co.™ (anchor color: Garnet `#8C2F26`)
Garnet is your "burgundy pivot." It appears on every product the company ever ships:
the Vejovis wordmark, the license page, the "not legal advice" disclaimer furniture,
the footer line. Small, constant, unmistakable — the thread through the series.

### Level 2 — The product: Court Ready™ (volume color: Pine `#1D4435`)
Pine is Court Ready's identity. Every Court Ready surface leads with Pine; Clay does
the work (steps, actions, numbers); Marigold gets exactly one highlight per section.

### Future volumes (the series play)
When you ship product #2, keep everything — fonts, neutrals, the mark, Garnet
house anchor, layout components — and swap **only the volume color**:

| Volume | Lead color | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Court Ready™ (custody) | Pine `#1D4435` | grounded, steady |
| e.g. eldercare / medical advocacy | Cloudy Blue-slate `#3E5C6B` | calm, clinical |
| e.g. estate / after-loss organizer | Toffee-brown `#8A6240` | warm, archival |

One variable changes; everything else holds. That is exactly the "distinct yet
undeniably related" effect you described.

## 3. The distinctive visual idea: **the Evidence Aesthetic**

Every competitor in the custody-binder space uses either (a) pastel mommy-blog
florals or (b) cold legal-tech SaaS blue. Your unfair advantage is that your product
*is* a records system — so the brand should look like **a beautifully kept case
file**. Make the marketing look like the product:

1. **The bracket-and-check is your logo.** The corner bracket (a form's corner) plus
   the clay check (an entry completed) is already drawn in CSS on every page header.
   Extract it, name it — *"the Record Mark"* — and use it as favicon, social avatar,
   watermark, and product-cover mark. It is simple, ownable, and means something:
   *captured and verified.*
2. **Mono-type metadata as decoration.** `EVT-0001_2026-07-18`, timestamps,
   `EXHIBIT A` tags, dotted index rows with leader lines. Use IBM Plex Mono
   labels as the signature texture on covers, mockups, and social posts. No one
   else in this niche looks like this.
3. **The Feelings → Facts flip.** Your `.compare` card (garnet "what you feel" vs
   pine "what you write") is the product's core transformation in one image. It is
   your best-performing social/ad format waiting to happen: endless before/after
   posts, each one teaching the method and selling the system.
4. **Paper, not screens.** Warm ground (`#FCFAF6`), one soft shadow, letter-size
   proportions. Product mockups should read as *printed documents on a desk* —
   because the end state of your product is a binder handed to an attorney.

### Do / Don't (locks in the "calm, organized, expensive" vibe)
- **Do** keep dark colors for text and accents only; backgrounds stay paper.
- **Do** allow Marigold exactly one appearance per page/section.
- **Do** write in the product voice everywhere: short, neutral, steady. No hype
  punctuation, no ALL-CAPS urgency, no emoji in product surfaces.
- **Don't** add new hues (no purples, no bright blues) — semantic colors only.
- **Don't** use stock photos of sad children or courtroom gavels. The brand is
  about the parent's competence, not the conflict.
- **Don't** use more than the three fonts. The trio *is* the look.
